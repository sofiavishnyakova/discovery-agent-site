The full workflow

How a discovery scan works

Eight stages, from the first consent notice to checking whether the new tool actually saved time. Every example on this page comes from our pilot with a simulated accounts-payable team.

1

Set up

Your managerEach participant

You choose which team to study and which web apps may be recorded. Everyone else stays out of it.

Each participant installs a browser extension and reads a plain consent notice before recording can start. Nobody is recorded without agreeing.

2

Record

Your team, working as usual

People do their normal job. The extension writes down what happens, step by step, like an observer with a stopwatch.

It logs which field someone typed in and how many characters, never the text itself. A small badge stays on screen with a Pause button.

3

Split into steps

Plain code

Hundreds of tiny events become a short list of steps a person would recognise.

Code drops the noise (stray clicks, repeated keystrokes) and cuts at natural boundaries. The originals are kept, so every step can be traced back.

AI

The AI writes one plain sentence per step, then finds the work that repeats across people and days.

Each recording is marked sensitive or not. Sensitive work is analysed on a computer in your office; anything combined with it stays there too.

5

Measure

Plain code

Code turns observed time into money, in three buckets: doing the work, fixing mistakes, and checking it.

Time per invoice comes from the recordings. Volume and hourly rates are assumptions, shown openly, that you confirm in the interview.

6

Recommend

Catalog + scoring

Each process is matched against a catalog of tools that already exist, scored by a published method, and checked for payback at your volume.

We don't build automations and take no referral fees. Where nothing fits, we write a spec your own team or agency can build from.

7

Confirm, then re-measure

AI draftsPeople decide

Every finding starts as a draft. A consultant or your manager confirms or rejects it, and the interview plan tests the assumptions before anyone buys a tool.

After the tool goes live, the same team records again, and you see whether the time really went down.
